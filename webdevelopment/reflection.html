Reflection

IT Support Guru

I am currently working as a Support Systems Administrator at Mizzou. In my role, I serve as the lead enterprise administrator for the university-wide Jamf Pro mobile device management platform, managing Apple hardware (macOS and iOS/iPadOS) across all academic colleges, units, and UM system affiliates. I collaborate with other IT professionals to establish, document, and govern enterprise IT standards, configuration profiles, and automated deployment pipelines for distributed IT teams throughout the campus network. ​

Reflecting on my previous jobs, I realize how much I have learned from those experiences; without them, I would not be where I am today. My first job in IT was with Gravity, where I worked as an Apple Technician. This position taught me a great deal, as it was a small business where I wore multiple hats, such as receptionist and technician. I learned to diagnose problems in front of customers and to remain calm in stressful situations. This job sparked my interest in focusing on Apple devices and helped me develop my skills. I was also fortunate to have colleagues from whom I could learn, which contributed significantly to my growth. ​

In my previous position, I had the opportunity to work with amazing colleagues and users, and I occasionally go back to visit them. This role taught me a lot about working with people who may not be as technically inclined. I learned to communicate with end users in a way that made my explanations understandable. This experience helped me recognize that everyone has strengths and areas of expertise, and when we collaborate effectively, we produce great results. ​

My career goal is to become a cloud architect or cloud engineer. That said, I would like to stay in my current position for a while longer because the work environment is excellent, and I have colleagues from whom I can learn. I have been building my skillset to meet the requirements for a cloud engineer role. I prefer to remain in a technical position, as I don't envision myself in a management role; the responsibilities of managing people, including possibly having to terminate employees, are challenging for me. I will continue to learn and strive to meet the requirements for my desired role while pushing forward in my career.​
